\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{Chapter::Abstract}

    % Replace with your own content
    Creep, the time-dependent deformation of metallic materials under sustained mechanical stress, plays a crucial role in the design and longevity of components operating at high temperatures, such as turbine blades and power plant structures. Recent advances in machine learning (ML) and numerical simulations offer promising alternatives for predicting creep behaviour more efficiently compared to the traditional approaches of experimental testing setups or complex numerical simulations.
           
    This thesis investigates the application of machine learning techniques to predict creep behaviour based on numerical simulations and experimental data. It takes a first step toward applying ML techniques to model and predict standard creep behaviour, aiming to improve data efficiency and support faster material evaluation. A key focus is on the representation of creep curves, the optimization of training datasets, and the development of surrogate models that can efficiently approximate numerical simulations. Different sampling techniques, including Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) and active learning strategies, are explored to enhance model performance and minimize computational costs. Feature extraction techniques, as well as knowledge-driven approaches from the field of materials science, are employed to reduce the dimensionality of creep test data and improve prediction accuracy.
    
    A classification model is developed to identify valid parameter ranges for simulations, and regression models (e.g., neural networks, random forests) are trained to map material parameters to creep curves. The effectiveness of active learning in improving model efficiency is evaluated against traditional random sampling approaches. Results indicate that ML-based models can provide accurate creep predictions.
    
    This study contributes to the broader goal of data-driven materials science, specifically within the AID4GREENEST project, by offering an efficient framework for creep prediction using machine learning. Future research will focus on extending this methodology to accelerated creep tests and integrating hybrid physics-informed ML models for further improvements in predictive accuracy.
    
    \noindent Keywords: Creep behaviour, machine learning, numerical simulation, active learning, data-driven materials science, creep curve representation, regression.
