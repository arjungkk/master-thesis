    Creep, the time-dependent deformation of materials under sustained stress, is critical in high-temperature applications such as power plants and aerospace components. Traditional analysis relies on long-term experiments and complex numerical simulations, which are often time-consuming and resource-intensive.
    
    Machine learning offers a promising alternative by enabling data-driven modeling, accelerating surrogate development, and supporting inverse parameter identification. This thesis takes a first step toward applying ML techniques to model and predict standard creep behavior, aiming to improve data efficiency and support faster, more cost-effective material evaluation.
    
    
      
    This thesis investigates the application of machine learning techniques to predict creep behavior based on numerical simulations and experimental data. A key focus is on the representation of creep curves, the optimization of training datasets, and the development of surrogate models that can efficiently approximate complex numerical simulations. Different sampling techniques—including Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) and active learning strategies—are explored to enhance model performance and minimize computational costs. Feature extraction techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Kernel PCA, and autoencoders are employed to reduce the dimensionality of creep test data and improve prediction accuracy.
    
    A classification model is developed to identify valid parameter ranges for simulations, and regression models (e.g., neural networks, random forests) are trained to map material parameters to creep curves. The effectiveness of active learning in improving model efficiency is evaluated against traditional random sampling approaches. Results indicate that ML-based models can provide accurate creep predictions while reducing computational overhead, making them a viable alternative to conventional methods.
    
    This study contributes to the broader goal of data-driven materials science, specifically within the AID4GREENEST project, by offering an efficient framework for creep prediction using machine learning. Future research will focus on extending this methodology to accelerated creep tests and integrating hybrid physics-informed ML models for further improvements in predictive accuracy.
    
    \noindent Keywords: Creep behavior, machine learning, numerical simulation, active learning, data-driven materials science, creep curve representation, regression modeling.
